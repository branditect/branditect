Where the colour is, and why it stops there

One gradient, at the top only. The orange-to-violet band is the brand mark's own gradient extended, and it runs once. Repeat it down the page and the fields stop being the subject — a settings screen is a form, and the form has to stay the loudest thing on it. The radial sweep in the corner is what keeps it from reading as a flat fill.

The four tiles are the hierarchy. Orange for you, sage for the brand, violet for language, navy for the account — each reusing a gradient already in tokens.css. You can find a section by colour before you have read a word, and nothing needed inventing to do it.

Language is two cards, not two rows. The layout carries the argument: one is labelled Sinulle in violet, the other Asiakkaillesi in orange. The single most common mistake here is assuming one switch does both, and a row-and-dropdown pair invites exactly that.

The coming rows have a violet edge, not grey. Flat grey reads as broken; a tinted edge reads as deliberate. Credit use carries a dimmed meter so the shape of the thing is visible before it works — still not focusable, still not clickable.
