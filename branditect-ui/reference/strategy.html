08

Boundaries

The section that stops the AI writing the wrong thing
We never
  • —Compete on price or run permanent discounts. It contradicts "honestly priced".
  • —Claim salon equivalence we can't evidence with a number.
  • —Use fear or insecurity about appearance to sell.
  • —Talk down to the reader about their own hair.
We always
  • +Lead with the result the customer gets, then the technology.
  • +Put a number behind a performance claim.
  • +Name the price plainly. It is an advantage, not an apology.
  • +Write the way we'd explain it to a friend.

Never compromise on
Performance · Accessibility · Design · Trust — if a piece of work trades one away, it is off-strategy regardless of how well it performs.

What changed, and why

Read this before implementing — the reasoning matters more than the pixels.

  • The page now opens with the answer. The old header said "Your brand strategy" and made you read eight cards to find the point. The orange hero leads with the difference statement, because that one line is what a new team member needs and what the AI cites most.
  • The brand pyramid is back, in the hero. It was promised in onboarding and then never shown again. It belongs where the strategy is read, narrowing from attributes to one idea — that narrowing is the argument.
  • "The whole strategy, in one paragraph". A generated prose version sits above the sections. Nobody forwards a dashboard to a new hire; they forward a paragraph. It's also the cheapest thing to stuff into a prompt.
  • Positioning gained "Not for". A positioning statement without an exclusion isn't a position, it's a description. Naming who you turn away is what stops it drifting.
  • Every pillar now carries proof. "Professional performance" is an adjective; "110,000 RPM" is a fact. Copy written from adjectives reads like every other brand — this is the single biggest upgrade to output quality.
  • Boundaries is new, and it's the most valuable section on the page. We never / We always, plus words we use and avoid. Generative tools are far better at avoiding a named mistake than at inferring taste. This section is what makes the difference between on-brand and generically competent.
  • Audience gained channels. Discovery copy leads with the result, decision copy leads with proof. Same strategy, different order — and the supporting messages are now tagged to match.
  • The competitor grid became rows with a price ladder, and Deklan sits in it. Three logo cards beside a map made you do the comparison yourself; a ladder with your own price in it makes the gap the point.
  • "Where Branditect uses this" closes the page. The strategy page's second job is being machine-readable, and nothing told the user that. If people know the AI reads it, they fill it in properly.
