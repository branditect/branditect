What this design decides

Reference for Studio ▸ Write. Colours are the Home gradients; no new families.

This file shows the content area only. In the app it renders inside app/(app)/layout.tsx — the left sidebar stays, same as every other page. Do not build a full-screen layout for this route.

  • Two required answers, everything else optional. Format and subject are steps 1 and 2; product, length and draft count sit under "Options" and are pre-filled. Someone who wants copy in ten seconds gets it, and someone who wants control has it in the same panel.
  • The brief stays on screen while drafts appear beside it. Writing is iterating on the brief, not filling a form once — a wizard that hides the brief behind the result forces a back-navigation on every attempt.
  • Examples are tappable and format-specific. "Warehouse closed for maintenance next week" teaches the shape of a good subject line faster than any helper text, and it changes when you switch format. Same principle as the onboarding placeholder matrix: show the standard, don't describe it.
  • "Something else — tell us what" is a real seventh option, not a hidden dropdown. The six named formats cover most work; the seventh is what stops the tool feeling like it can only do six things.
  • Save, not Pin. Saved drafts go to Studio ▸ Library, which is the one home for saved outputs — the same word the nav already uses. Library carries a count in the header so the button means something before you've clicked it.
  • Three drafts by default, not one. Choosing between options is faster and more honest than judging a single draft in isolation, and it stops the user editing the first thing they see out of politeness.
  • Every draft shows what it drew on. "Fact: 12× weight, from Product range specs" is the line that makes the output trustworthy — and it's how someone spots a claim that came from nowhere. It's the Knowledge page's closed-book promise, kept at the point of output.
  • The source line under the button says what it can't do. "It won't invent a fact that isn't in there" sets the expectation before generation, which is where expectations actually get set.
  • Streaming draft shows a status, not a spinner. "Checking every claim against your product records" tells the user what the wait is buying them.
